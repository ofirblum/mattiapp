# First-Session Refinement

## Scope and Evidence

This refines the existing local prototype. It does not create a new application,
infer a psychological diagnosis, configure a backend or publish private records.
The parent reports recurring algebraic sign mistakes and high confidence despite
incorrect results. Those observations justify targeted practice, not assumptions
about every algebra skill or the cause of an individual answer.

## Mathematical Input

MathLive supplies real horizontal fractions with editable numerator/denominator,
brackets, negative values, algebra, nested fractions, undo/redo and a mathematical
keyboard. Each mathematical answer and checkpoint has its own fraction control.
The working area can append a visual rekenregel without forcing a slash-only
method. Entries resume in structural ASCII form and are rendered as mathematics.
Fonts are included locally; no cloud tutor or CDN is required.

Grading validates a bounded AST before evaluation. Numeric operations and complete
constant denominator expressions are accepted. Polynomial equivalence uses exact
Nerdamer expansion, not one sampled numeric value. Algebraic denominators can be
entered and saved, but automatic grading does not certify their domains or
equivalence outside supported cases. Those go to review rather than false failure.

## Sign Sequence and Calibration

Fourteen original variants cover bracket subtraction, negated differences,
negative products, signed like terms, fractional coefficients and longer mixed
expressions. The supplied examples have tested solutions. Known wrong sign forms
receive a named-step correction, concise rule and concrete counterexample;
unknown incorrect answers are not confidently assigned a misconception.

When useful, the student evaluates both the original and their proposed
expression with the same numbers. For `-(a-b)=-a-b`, a=5 and b=2 give -3 and -7.
The two values are entered by the student and compared; this is a counterexample
to equivalence, not a general proof of equality. The student edits their own
expression afterward. The correction and verification remain separate events.

A fresh related question follows a confirmed sign error when there is space,
with a seven-item cap. Prior hints/feedback on the original do not contaminate
the fresh item's first attempt. Existing six-question unfinished sessions gain
one sign-priority item while retaining their original IDs and working. A full
seven-question session is not expanded further; later review remains available.

Stage 2 requires two correct first unassisted expansions on distinct items.
Stage 3 requires four on at least three items across two Brussels dates. The
mixed stage does not announce which sign rule is being tested. Recent confirmed
sign errors increase selection priority for fourteen days, not permanently.
One priority sign item is selected alongside numbers, geometry, application and
writing; the rest of the curriculum is not replaced. Timing remains approximate
and should be tuned from actual use without a countdown or speed pressure.

Confidence is optional on focused items, recorded before feedback and frozen
once feedback occurs. The parent sees first-expansion accuracy and recent/prior
samples, confirmed recurring errors, confidence mismatches and verification
counts. These measurements do not certify whole-topic mastery. Final answers
after checks, explanations, grouping feedback or retries remain assisted.
